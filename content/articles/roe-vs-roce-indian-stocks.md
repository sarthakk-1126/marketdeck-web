## ROE and ROCE answer different accounting questions

Return on equity (ROE) compares profit after tax with shareholders' equity. Return on capital employed (ROCE) compares operating profit with a broader capital base. For a beginner, the most useful approach is to read both ratios while keeping their formulas and reporting periods consistent.

For this guide, ROE means profit after tax divided by average shareholders' equity. ROCE means EBIT divided by average capital employed, where capital employed is total assets minus current liabilities. EBIT means earnings before interest and tax. These are stated analytical conventions; other providers may use different definitions.

The IFRS Conceptual Framework describes equity as the residual interest in assets after deducting liabilities. [S3] NSE financial-result filings also distinguish reporting periods and standalone from consolidated results, which matters when building ratio inputs. [S2]

## A simple worked example

Imagine two hypothetical manufacturers. Each employs ₹1,000 crore of capital and earns ₹180 crore of EBIT, so each has an 18% ROCE under the convention above.

Company A is funded entirely with ₹1,000 crore of equity. Company B uses ₹400 crore of equity and ₹600 crore of debt. If Company B pays 8% interest, its annual interest expense is ₹48 crore. Assuming a 25% tax rate for this teaching example, Company A has ₹135 crore of profit after tax while Company B has ₹99 crore.

Company A's ROE is therefore 13.5%. Company B's ROE is 24.75%.

The operating return was the same, but the financing mix changed the equity denominator and the profit available after interest. That is why ROE and ROCE should not be treated as interchangeable scores.

## The gap needs explanation

A large difference between ROE and ROCE is a prompt to inspect debt, finance cost, taxes, non-operating income, cash balances and changes in equity. It is not a mechanical diagnosis.

SEBI's investor due-diligence guidance recommends examining the cash-flow statement, income statement and balance sheet and comparing companies with competitors. [S1] That broader approach helps prevent one ratio from carrying more meaning than it should.

## Use consistent periods

A period profit is often compared with a balance-sheet amount measured at a point in time. Average opening and closing balances can therefore be more informative than closing balances alone.

If a hypothetical company starts with ₹500 crore of equity, ends with ₹700 crore and earns ₹120 crore of profit after tax, average equity is ₹600 crore. ROE on average equity is 20%, while ROE on closing equity is about 17.1%. The profit did not change; only the denominator convention changed.

## A practical checklist

Before comparing ROE and ROCE, verify the reporting period, standalone versus consolidated basis, formula, multi-year trend, debt and finance cost. Then connect the return ratios to cash flow and valuation rather than treating either percentage as a conclusion.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is ROCE better than ROE?

Not universally. ROCE is useful for understanding operating returns on a broader capital base, while ROE focuses on profit relative to shareholder equity.

### Can ROE be higher than ROCE?

Yes. Financing structure, taxes, non-operating items and different numerator definitions can all contribute.

### Is there one good percentage?

No universal threshold works across sectors and cycles. Consistent definitions, multi-year trends and comparable businesses matter more than a single cut-off.

### Should banks be read the same way?

Use caution. Funding is part of the operating model of banks and many lenders, so an industrial-company ROCE convention may not be comparable. Use sector-appropriate measures and regulated disclosures.
