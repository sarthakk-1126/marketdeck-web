## Turn five perspectives into one research record

A practical stock research checklist should connect the business, valuation, price history, market conversation and unresolved risks without treating them as five independent votes. Its purpose is to make a conclusion traceable: which facts support it, which assumptions it depends on, and what evidence could change it.

The framework in this article is MarketDeck's proposed research method, not a validated stock-picking system. It is designed for an India-focused reader who wants to move beyond collecting screenshots and headlines. It does not assign buy or sell ratings or promise that completing a checklist removes investment risk.

The most useful output is a short memo backed by a source register. Someone revisiting it later should be able to distinguish what you knew, what you inferred and what you still needed to investigate. That is a more durable research asset than a confident opinion with no record of its inputs.

## Start with one question and a boundary

Write the question before opening more tabs. For example: is a manufacturer's reported growth supported by repeatable operating cash generation? That question suggests particular statements and periods to inspect. Is this the best stock is too vague to determine what would count as evidence.

Define the universe and timeframe. A sector comparison needs broadly comparable businesses and accounting periods. An event study needs an information-availability boundary. A long-term business question and a short-term price question can coexist, but they should not be combined without explaining the different horizons.

Choose a stopping rule for the first pass. You might aim to identify the three main drivers, verify the important inputs and list the unresolved issues. This is a proposed workflow, not a mandatory numerical standard. Its value is preventing endless browsing from being mistaken for deeper understanding.

Create a document register with a title, source URL, period, publication or availability date, retrieval date and purpose. NSE's announcements and financial-results resources provide primary routes to issuer disclosures. [S1] [S2] The register should distinguish the original filing from commentary about it.

## Lens 1: What does the business actually do?

Describe who pays the company, what they receive and which resources the company needs to provide it. Identify the economic unit that matters: a subscription, tonne, store, loan or project can imply very different operating models. Do not begin with a ratio before understanding the numerator's business meaning.

Then map the main financial statements to the question. The SEC's introductory guide distinguishes financial position, performance and cash movements across the statements. That is useful conceptual background, not a substitute for the applicable Indian reporting framework or the issuer's notes. [S3]

In a hypothetical manufacturer, revenue growth might be accompanied by higher receivables and inventory. The business question is whether those investments support future sales and collections or signal deterioration in conversion. The [Indian stock-screening guide](/intelligence/notes/business-before-the-stock/) works through the arithmetic and the questions to ask next.

Write a business explanation that could be wrong. For instance: the recent increase in working capital is expected to reverse as the new product cycle matures. Then identify the next evidence needed—collections, inventory movement or later cash flow. A claim that cannot be challenged is difficult to learn from.

## Lens 2: What expectations are in the valuation?

Valuation research asks how price relates to a stated economic measure or scenario. It does not ask whether a company is good in the abstract. A resilient business can still be priced for an optimistic future, while an apparently cheap multiple can reflect a fragile denominator.

Record the price date and earnings or cash-flow period together. Distinguish trailing results from forecasts, and stated forecasts from your own scenarios. A scenario is useful because it makes assumptions visible; it should not be presented as though the company has already delivered it.

Suppose a hypothetical share costs ₹240 and annual earnings per share are ₹12. Its P/E is 20 under that basis. If sustainable earnings were instead ₹8, the multiple would be 30. The key research question is not which arithmetic you prefer but what evidence supports the sustainable-earnings assumption.

Keep at least one meaningful downside explanation. What could cause margins, reinvestment returns or cash generation to disappoint? Avoid a false sense of precision from a spreadsheet with many decimal places. The model is only as informative as its assumptions and the evidence supporting them.

## Lens 3: What has price done, under which settings?

Price history can describe the market path around the business question. It can show drawdowns, recoveries and relative performance within a chosen window. It cannot independently verify accounting quality or explain the intentions of the people who traded.

Save instrument identity, interval, dates, scale and corporate-action treatment. Our [chart-reading guide](/intelligence/notes/a-chart-is-a-question/) explains why linear versus logarithmic scales, dividends and rebasing matter. Two screenshots with different settings may suggest different stories without either being factually incorrect.

Tie event claims to publication timing. A company can report a quarter weeks after the period ends. Do not use the period-end date as the time when the market learned the information. The [NSE filing guide](/intelligence/notes/read-nse-company-announcements-results/) provides a simple three-clock record for period, availability and retrieval.

Keep the description separate from the proposed cause. Price rose after a release is an observation about sequence. The release caused the rise requires a stronger argument and consideration of other information. Good research becomes more precise when these statements occupy different lines in the memo.

## Lens 4: What is the market conversation adding?

Commentary can reveal competing explanations, assumptions and questions that a numerical screen misses. The useful task is not to count positive and negative words and stop. It is to understand what claim is being made, who is making it, what evidence they offer and whether the claim adds anything new.

Trace repeated stories back to their origin. If three commentators discuss the same company announcement, you have three interpretations of one disclosure, not necessarily three independent confirmations. If all quote the same analyst, the information chain may be narrower still.

For a relevant statement, save the exact source location or timestamp and enough surrounding context to preserve its meaning. A conditional sentence such as margins could improve if input costs remain stable should not become margins will improve. The condition is part of the claim.

Use [MarketDeck Commentary](/commentary/) to explore available source discussions and context. Then compare the narrative with the original filing. Keep disagreement visible: one view may focus on demand while another focuses on capital intensity. You do not need to average them into a single sentiment score before understanding the difference.

## Lens 5: What could make the analysis fail?

Risk review should challenge the research process as well as the business. Ask about financing, customer concentration, cyclicality, governance, liquidity and execution where relevant. Also ask whether the dataset is incomplete, a ratio is misdefined or a key claim depends on a source you have not inspected.

Separate uncertainty from a known negative fact. Missing segment detail is not evidence of misconduct. Equally, an unresolved gap should not be presented as a confirmed strength. Use clear labels such as confirmed, reported by management, inferred and unresolved.

Write an invalidation condition in observable terms. Instead of saying we will watch the situation, specify which future evidence would weaken the view. For the working-capital example, that might be continued deterioration in collections across subsequent comparable periods. The exact condition depends on the hypothesis; it is not a universal investment rule.

Do not convert the checklist into a guarantee of safety. Research reduces some avoidable errors but cannot eliminate unexpected events or market risk. A complete-looking document can still contain poor assumptions, which is why the sources and challenge process remain visible.

## Use an evidence matrix, not a confidence score

A matrix forces each claim to earn its place. Start with the claim, then list the strongest available source, its timestamp, the relevant measurement or passage and the remaining limitation. Avoid assigning an unexplained numerical confidence percentage simply to make the memo look quantitative.

| Hypothetical claim | Evidence to inspect | Limitation to retain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Revenue growth is broad-based | Comparable segment results and notes | Aggregate growth may hide different segment trends |
| Cash conversion is improving | Operating cash flow and working-capital movements | One period can be seasonal |
| Valuation assumes durable earnings | Stated price and earnings scenarios | Sustainable earnings remain an estimate |
| Commentary confirms the story | Original source behind each discussion | Repetition may not be independent evidence |
| The event explains a price move | Dated release and aligned price history | Sequence alone does not establish causation |

The matrix should distinguish source quality from claim correctness. A primary filing is the right place to find what the company reported, but it does not make every forward-looking statement a certainty. A secondary explanation can be insightful while still needing verification of its underlying inputs.

Also record contradictory evidence. If a source weakens the initial view, do not remove it from the collection merely because it complicates the narrative. A memo that preserves the strongest objection is more useful to its future reader than one optimised to win an argument today.

## Assemble a one-page research memo

Begin with the question and a short provisional conclusion. Follow with the three facts that matter most, each linked to its source. Then list the assumptions connecting those facts to your interpretation. Put unresolved questions and invalidation conditions immediately after the conclusion, not in an obscure appendix.

Attach calculations where necessary. A ratio should show its inputs, units, period and definition. A chart should show its settings. A quotation should point to its source context. The one-page limit applies to the argument, not to the audit trail supporting it.

End with the next research action. That could be reading a specific note, checking a later filing or resolving a metric mismatch. It need not be a trade. A decision to wait for clearer evidence is a legitimate research outcome rather than a failure to produce a recommendation.

Use [StockProof](/screener/) for available fundamental exploration, [Charting](/charts/) for price context and [F&O](/futures-and-options/) where derivatives information is relevant. Do not force every tool into every memo. A lens earns its place by answering part of the question, not because it is available on the homepage.

## Review the process after new information arrives

When you revisit the memo, compare the new evidence with the original expectations before editing the old text. Preserve dated versions so you can see whether the reasoning improved or merely adapted to the outcome. This is particularly important when a favourable price move tempts you to rewrite an initially weak argument.

Review errors by type. Did you extract the wrong value, misunderstand the business, use an inappropriate comparison or overstate what a source supported? Different errors require different repairs. Adding more indicators will not fix a habit of confusing standalone and consolidated data.

AI can help organise documents or draft summaries, but verify its citations and calculations before treating them as evidence. The guide to [evaluating AI trading agents](/intelligence/notes/evaluate-ai-trading-agents/) explains why task performance, financial results and operational reliability should be tested separately.

The goal is a process that becomes more explicit and more reproducible over time. More pages, more tabs and more certainty are not inherently signs of progress. Better questions, cleaner measurements and a clearer record of what could be wrong are.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do all five lenses need to agree?

No. They answer different questions and may point in different directions. A business can improve while its valuation becomes demanding, or a price trend can strengthen while an important accounting question remains unresolved. Preserve the disagreement and investigate it rather than manufacturing a consensus.

### How many sources are enough for stock research?

There is no useful universal count. Start with the primary documents required to verify the important claims, then add sources that contribute independent information or meaningful challenge. Several articles repeating one disclosure should not be treated as several independent observations.

### Can I use this checklist for crypto or AI research?

The habits of defining a question, checking sources and recording uncertainty transfer well, but the underlying measurements and risks must change. See the [crypto fundamentals guide](/intelligence/notes/crypto-fundamentals-fees-revenue-token-value/) for why protocol revenue and token rights cannot simply be treated like ordinary company earnings.
