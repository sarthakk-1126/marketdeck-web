THE MARKETDECK BRIEF / FIELDNOTES 03 / PAGE 1

AI in Indian Finance

Where it is actually creating value - and why the important boundary is not AI versus humans, but assistance versus delegated authority.

The working thesis

The most defensible near-term value is showing up in workflow compression: service, document processing, fraud detection, risk support, research and operations. The higher the consequence of an automated decision, the stronger the case for provenance, human review and clear accountability.

Published 28 September 2026. Research and education only. This issue does not provide investment, credit or legal advice.

00 / THE RESEARCH MAP / PAGE 2

Useful AI is mostly workflow AI

Ignore the science-fiction framing. In Indian finance, the practical story is already visible in the operating layer.

Five places to look

  • Customer service: faster routing, retrieval and first-line resolution.
  • Fraud and surveillance: pattern detection that narrows what humans need to inspect.
  • Lending and credit: document processing and decision support around underwriting.
  • Research and compliance: summarisation, monitoring, document review and analyst assistance.
  • Operations: reconciliation, claims, voice-to-data conversion and employee copilots.

The control question

Automation can reduce friction without removing accountability. This issue separates low-consequence assistance from decisions that can deny credit, freeze money, shape advice or materially affect a customer. [S4] [S6] [S8]

Evidence note

Regulatory documents are treated as primary policy evidence. Company annual reports are used only for clearly labelled company-reported operating metrics. Drafts and consultations are labelled as drafts or consultations, not final rules.

Read the issue as a map of value plus control: what AI can accelerate, what must remain reviewable, and who remains responsible.

01 / THE VALUE MAP / PAGE 3

Five workflows, one pattern

The recurring pattern is not autonomous finance. It is AI reducing the amount of information a person or system must manually process before the next decision.

The useful middle

SEBI's own inventory of market use cases includes surveillance, KYC/AML, risk management, data analysis, document review, cyber security, customer service and chatbots. RBI examples include fraud detection and complaint handling. [S2] [S5] [S7]

Where value compounds

AI becomes more useful when the input is high-volume, repetitive and reviewable: documents, conversations, transaction patterns, complaints, filings and internal knowledge. That does not make the downstream decision low-risk.

What this chart is - and is not

The visual is a MarketDeck workflow map, not a ranking of returns or an estimate of market size. It shows where primary sources document actual use or deployment.

Editorial takeaway: the strongest evidence today is around throughput and decision support, not autonomous financial judgment.

02 / CUSTOMER SERVICE / PAGE 4

The first win is response capacity

Service is a natural entry point because a bot can retrieve, classify and resolve routine work while preserving an escalation path for higher-stakes cases.

A concrete operating metric

Bajaj Finserv reports that 65% of customer servicing at Bajaj Finance was handled through AI voice and text bots in FY2026. It also reports that Bajaj Finserv Health resolved about 55% of customer queries using agentic AI. These are company-reported operating metrics, not independent quality scores. [S9]

The regulator is experimenting too

RBI's 2024-25 Annual Report says it is working with ReBIT to integrate AI into the Complaint Management System in phases: phase I for a conversational AI chatbot for complainants, followed by more advanced complaint-processing features. [S2]

Design the handoff, not just the bot

A service system should know when it is answering a routine question, when it is interpreting a rule, and when the customer needs a human. The practical KPI is not simply containment rate; it is resolution quality with a reliable escalation path.

Good service AI reduces waiting and repetitive work. It should not make a difficult complaint harder to reach a human with.

03 / FRAUD & SURVEILLANCE / PAGE 5

AI is useful when it narrows the haystack

Fraud systems are a strong fit for machine learning because the first job is often to surface unusual behaviour quickly - not to declare guilt.

MuleHunter.AI is a useful example

RBI described MuleHunter.AI, developed by RBI Innovation Hub, as an AI/ML model for detecting mule bank accounts more efficiently. A pilot with two large public sector banks produced encouraging results, and banks were encouraged to collaborate further. [S3]

Surveillance is already a securities-market use case

SEBI's board material lists surveillance, KYC/AML, risk management, behaviour analysis and cyber security among observed AI/ML uses across brokers, depositories, market infrastructure institutions and mutual funds. [S5]

The decision boundary matters

A model can rank suspicious accounts or transactions without becoming the final authority. False positives have real costs: blocked payments, delayed access, additional verification and customer distress. The higher the impact, the stronger the case for documented review and redress.

Detection is not adjudication. The safest architecture separates anomaly scoring from the decision that affects the customer.

04 / LENDING & CREDIT / PAGE 6

Faster underwriting is valuable - but consequential

Lending combines exactly what AI likes - documents, repeated decisions and large datasets - with exactly what governance worries about: fairness, explainability and access to credit.

AI is already connected to loan workflows

Bajaj Finserv reports Rs 5,520 crore of conversational-AI-enabled loan disbursals at Bajaj Finance in FY2026 and more than 69 million application documents processed using Vision AI. 'AI-enabled' should not be read as 'AI made the credit decision by itself'. [S9]

Document automation is not the same as credit judgment

Extracting data from applications, checking completeness and routing cases can remove manual work. Credit approval, pricing and adverse decisions introduce different risks because model errors can directly change a customer's access to finance.

Model risk is moving to the centre

RBI's June 2026 draft model-risk guidance expands the frame beyond credit models to all models used by covered regulated entities, including third-party and AI/ML models, across the model lifecycle. The document is a draft released for consultation, not final regulation. [S4]

The more a model changes a customer's economic outcome, the less adequate 'the model said so' becomes as an explanation.

05 / RESEARCH, ADVICE & COMPLIANCE / PAGE 7

AI can compress research; responsibility does not compress

Financial research is a high-leverage use case because models can search, summarise and compare. But the output can travel into advice, execution and compliance decisions.

SEBI documents broad use across the market

SEBI's 2025 consultation paper records AI/ML use by exchanges for surveillance, cyber security, member support and data automation; by brokers for KYC/document processing, recommendations, chatbots, account opening, surveillance, AML and order execution; and by mutual funds for customer support, surveillance, cyber security and segmentation. [S7]

Accountability follows the regulated activity

SEBI's 2025 Intermediaries Amendment Regulations make covered regulated persons solely responsible for investor and stakeholder data, the outputs from AI/ML tools they rely on, and compliance with applicable law - whether the tools are built internally or sourced from third parties. [S6]

Advice adds disclosure duties

SEBI's January 2025 investment-adviser guidelines state that advisers using AI tools remain responsible for client data, other information used to arrive at advice, AI-based advice and legal compliance, and must disclose the extent of AI use to clients. [S8]

AI may shorten the path from document to insight. It does not outsource fiduciary or regulatory responsibility.

06 / OPERATIONS / PAGE 8

The quiet gains are in the back office

Operations is where AI can create large cumulative value without needing to pretend it can forecast markets.

Voice and documents become structured data

Bajaj Finserv reports 52 million voice-to-data conversions for customer conversations at Bajaj Finance in FY2026, alongside more than 69 million application documents processed using Vision AI. [S9]

Claims and servicing become more automatable

The same annual report says Bajaj General expects about a 67% reduction in turnaround time for motor claims through automated invoice processing. Because the figure is explicitly an expected reduction, this issue does not present it as an achieved outcome. [S9]

Internal copilots can scale institutional knowledge

Axis Bank's FY2024-25 annual report says Axis Deep Intelligence, its GenAI internal chatbot, was deployed across more than 5,500 branches and supported more than 100,000 employees. That is an internal productivity deployment, not a claim about investment returns or customer outcomes. [S10]

The back office is a powerful proving ground: repetitive enough to automate, structured enough to measure, and close enough to human review to recover from errors.

07 / INDIA'S GOVERNANCE DIRECTION / PAGE 9

Enable innovation, keep responsibility visible

India's current policy direction is not a simple ban-or-permit choice. It increasingly combines encouragement of AI adoption with explicit responsibility for outcomes.

FREE-AI is a framework, not a marketing slogan

RBI's FREE-AI committee report sets seven foundational Sutras, 26 actionable recommendations and six strategic pillars. The RBI says the aim is to encourage innovation in harmony with risk mitigation. [S1]

SEBI puts responsibility on the regulated person

Under SEBI's 2025 Intermediaries Amendment Regulations, covered intermediaries remain responsible for data privacy/security/integrity, AI outputs they use, and legal compliance even when technology is procured from a third party. [S6]

Policy status matters

The FREE-AI report is a committee framework. SEBI's responsible-AI paper is a consultation paper. RBI's 2026 model-risk guidance is a draft. This issue labels each accordingly and does not describe proposals as binding final rules. [S1] [S4] [S7]

The emerging principle is durable: innovation can be delegated to tools; accountability cannot be delegated away.

08 / MODEL RISK & HUMAN OVERSIGHT / PAGE 10

The hard problem begins when the model can act

A summariser and an autonomous decision engine should not share the same control model. Consequence should determine governance.

RBI is broadening the model-risk lens

RBI's June 2026 draft guidance covers governance, risk tiering, lifecycle management, validation, continuous oversight, change management and business continuity across models used by covered regulated entities, including third-party and AI/ML models. [S4]

Think in levels of authority

  • Assist: retrieve, classify, summarise or draft. A human remains the decision-maker.
  • Recommend: score, rank or propose an action. Human review is explicit for material outcomes.
  • Act within bounds: execute only inside predetermined limits with logs, overrides and monitoring.
  • High-impact autonomous action: demands the strongest evidence, controls, accountability and redress.

Explainability is a control surface

The right explanation depends on the use case. A customer may need the reason an adverse decision occurred; a model-risk team needs reproducibility, validation and monitoring; an operations team needs failure modes and a rollback path. One generic 'AI explanation' cannot serve all three.

A useful rule: as reversibility falls and customer impact rises, human oversight and evidence requirements should rise with it.

09 / PRACTICAL CONTROL CHECKLIST / PAGE 11

Before automating a financial decision

Use this page as a research worksheet. The goal is not to slow every deployment; it is to make authority, evidence and recovery explicit before the system goes live.

Eight questions

  • What exact task is the model performing - retrieval, recommendation or action?
  • What data is it allowed to use, and what is the provenance and freshness of that data?
  • What financial or customer consequence can follow from a wrong output?
  • Who owns the decision and who can override it?
  • What explanation is required for staff, auditors, regulators and customers?
  • How are bias, drift, edge cases and adversarial inputs tested?
  • What is monitored after deployment, and what triggers rollback or shutdown?
  • What grievance or human-review path exists for an affected customer?

The operating principle

Do not evaluate only model accuracy. Evaluate the complete decision system: data, model, tools, people, controls, interfaces, monitoring and recovery.

A production AI system is a socio-technical control loop. Governance belongs around the whole loop, not only around the model.

10 / SOURCES & METHODOLOGY / PAGE 12

Primary sources first

Every material claim in this issue traces to a regulator document or a clearly labelled company annual report.

How evidence was handled

RBI and SEBI publications are treated as primary policy sources. Company annual reports are used for company-reported operating metrics and are not independently verified by MarketDeck. No company metric is used as proof that one AI system is superior to another.

Status labels are part of the evidence

A committee report, consultation paper and draft guidance are identified as such. They are not described as final binding rules unless the cited source is a final regulation or circular.

No investment conclusion

This issue evaluates operating use cases, controls and governance. It does not recommend securities, lenders, banks, fintechs or AI vendors and does not estimate investment returns from AI adoption.

Source review completed 28 September 2026. Recheck regulatory status before relying on any draft or consultation as current law.
